Why one word

12 August 2026

Every journaling app I ever installed died the same way. Not dramatically — quietly. Day one, three paragraphs. Day four, one paragraph, mostly apology. Day nine, the notification arrives and I feel a small, specific guilt, and I swipe it away. The app stays on my phone for another year as a monument to the person I briefly intended to be.

The problem was never discipline. The problem is that a blank page is a demand. "How was your day?" is an essay question, and nobody has an essay in them at 11pm.

So Kodama asks for one word.

Not a sentence. Not "at least a few lines." One word — heavy, okay, sunlit, thin, loud. Whatever is true. It takes about four seconds, which happens to be roughly the gap between brushing your teeth and deciding to do nothing else today.

Here's what I've learned watching my own months grow: one word is not a compromise on journaling. It's a different instrument. A journal entry describes a day; a single word distills it. Choosing between "tired" and "empty" turns out to be the whole act of reflection, compressed. Some nights the choosing takes longer than writing a paragraph would have — not because it's hard, but because it's honest.

And then the word does something, which is the part I actually built: it grows a tree. Every entry becomes growth — a branch reaching, leaves opening, some days a few petals. A month of your words becomes a tree that only you can read, shaped like what you actually lived. At the end of the month it joins your grove, and a new seed goes in the ground.

There's no streak counter. The only number in the app counts the words you've given this month — it counts up, never against you. If you skip four days, nothing is lost and nothing scolds you; the tree simply waited. It turns out you come back for a living thing in a way you never come back for a red badge.

One word. That's the whole ask. Small enough to say every day, true enough to mean something in a year.
